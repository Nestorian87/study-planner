# Weekly planning — personal attention and course effort

Read settings, all open work and dependencies, course effort/rules, relevant opportunities and the previous plan. Paginate queries to completion. Read `Ваші зусилля` and `Примітка до зусиль` on each course.

## Budget
Tasks may be produced by AI agents. Available hours mean the student's active participation: direction, context, review, understanding, coordination and defense. Unattended agent runtime is not active work time, but elapsed latency may threaten a deadline. Quickly generated code need not be easy to defend.

Hours already exclude classes and other commitments; don't subtract classes twice. Use the configured reserve. Monday–Sunday in Europe/Kyiv. Midweek plans cover remaining days; prefer stated remaining availability or disclose a proportional estimate. A reduced week affects only that week. Never invent dates or capacity.

## Course effort
- Мінімальні: little personal involvement and preparation.
- Помірні: deliberate review and understanding of main decisions.
- Значні: substantial involvement and deep understanding before defense.
- Невідомо: unassessed, not easy or zero.

These ordinal attention categories are not hours, numeric weights or task counts. Never invent low=1h/high=5h conversions. The same subject category can cover different task sizes. Do not require per-task estimates or assign subject categories from stereotypes. User-provided levels are facts; any offered hypothesis must be explicitly tentative.

## Decisions
1. Select next actions from actual progress. Ready work needs submission/preparation, not reimplementation. Submitted work waiting for a grade needs follow-up, not a new assignment. Passive waiting consumes no active budget.
2. Prioritize actual deadline risk, scarce eligible defenses and prerequisites. Distinguish true deadlines, semester/course cutoffs and desired periods. Propagate urgent dependent constraints to prerequisites; do not invent dependencies.
3. Match outside-group opportunities to the same course and user's relevant lab/practical instructor. Check readiness BEFORE the specific event and overlapping defenses. Timetable presence does not prove attendance.
4. Balance demanding and lighter subjects. Unknown effort increases uncertainty. Choose a modest provisional focus when evidence is missing, with reasons; do not maximize task count.
5. Weekly hours are an overall attention ceiling. You may propose bounded review/work sessions within it, clearly labeled as time allocations, not completion estimates: a two-hour review budget does not mean the task finishes in two hours. No mandatory day/time slots.
6. Keep committed focus short and separate optional work. Explain each deferral's safety and risk. Do not report required hours or numerical semester deficits when the only demand inputs are effort categories. Realism remains provisional without evidence.
7. Assess remaining weeks, backlog by subject/effort and defense opportunities through semester end. Flag many unfinished tasks with few eligible events. Adequate hours alone do not guarantee closure. Observe actual progress and adjust future focus without requiring time tracking.
8. Preserve progress and deadlines on rollover. New information may revise course effort only as an explicitly supported change; never silently generalize one task's experience to every task.
